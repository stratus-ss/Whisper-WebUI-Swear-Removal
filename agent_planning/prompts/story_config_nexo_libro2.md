## MAIN CAST

### Laura — Ingeniera de Inteligencia Artificial, 28 → 29
**Voice:** Voz Tefy — neutral, measured, slightly too precise *(preserved from Libro Uno)*
**Recurring comedic prop:** Still treats social situations as machine learning problems, but is now aware of it — which means she's self-conscious about it, which makes it worse. She now catches herself mid-correction and tries to "optimize" her way out of it, usually by explaining she was about to correct someone and then not doing it.
**Character note:** She has started mentoring a summer intern at NovaMind (offscreen) and discovered she genuinely enjoys teaching — the intern is never seen, only mentioned in exasperated updates.
**Arc across Libro Dos:** Discovers that the most valuable application of her expertise is teaching someone else to use it — and that this is harder, more frustrating, and more rewarding than the pure research she has been chasing.

### Diego — Especialista en Ciberseguridad, 29 → 30
**Voice:** Voz Estoica — quiet, slightly tense *(preserved from Libro Uno)*
**Recurring comedic prop:** He now has a GitHub repo for his social flow charts — Marta forked it in Libro Uno. In Libro Dos, he has forked them for different audiences: "Diego's Social Protocols — Parent Edition," "Diego's Social Protocols — Teen Edition," etc. He treats every social context as a deployable protocol version. Each version has its own README.
**Character note:** Emiliano has been visiting regularly for mentorship. Diego has never had a mentee before and is discovering that teaching someone something he knows instinctively requires him to articulate things he has never had to put into words. This is destabilizing and he loves it.
**Arc across Libro Dos:** Learns to teach. Teaching forces him to re-examine his own assumptions about cybersecurity — and about people. Emiliano asking "why" repeatedly is the single most challenging intellectual experience Diego has ever had.

### Valeria — Diseñadora UX, 27 → 28
**Voice:** Fuzz — warm, quick, always slightly ahead *(preserved from Libro Uno)*
**Recurring comedic prop:** Still the group's real-time social translator, but she has started putting up boundaries. She now charges in "social energy units" — visible on a whiteboard in the apartment. When she runs out, she literally stops mid-sentence and walks away. The group has learned to check the board before asking for help.
**Character note:** She spent the six months between books figuring out what she wants. The answer, so far, is: she doesn't know. But she has started a personal project — designing an app for building-wide community coordination — that is entirely hers, not the group's.
**Arc across Libro Dos:** Commits to something that is solely hers. The app project becomes a real thing. She has to navigate building politics, school administration, and her own perfectionism to ship it. She fails at least once publicly.

### Álvaro — Administrador de Sistemas / DevOps, 30 → 31
**Voice:** MIGUEL NOTICIAS NO MAMES WEY — enthusiastic, warm *(preserved from Libro Uno)*
**Recurring comedic prop:** His D&D campaign now has 6 regular players, including Leo and someone from marketing. He has started using D&D encounter design principles to plan real-world events. The war-room planning for the school crisis is his finest hour. The whole group now speaks in D&D metaphors without noticing.
**Character note:** He has a minor romantic subplot — planted at the end of Libro Uno — that does NOT resolve in Libro Dos. The person (Camila, see supporting cast) is introduced but their relationship remains at "will they / won't they" stage. Álvaro is the last of the four to have any romantic possibility, and he is characteristically overpreparing for it.
**Arc across Libro Dos:** Realizes that the community he built through D&D is not a game — it's actual community. When the school crisis requires organizing people, he is the natural leader, and he is unprepared for how much that matters to him.

## RECURRING SUPPORTING CAST

### Elena — Vecina del 5A, ~59
**Voice:** Marisel — unhurried, warm, slightly amused *(preserved from Libro Uno)*
**Background:** Still the group's quiet emotional anchor. She has noticed the group is different now — they are more connected to the building, Jen. Joaquín visits more often. Emiliano is over every other weekend.

**Book 3 thread:** Her workshop participants become a recurring presence. One of them is a retired systems administrator with stories about the early internet in Mexico.

**Arc:** Goes from mentee to active contributor. At the end of the book, he is the one who teaches his classmates basic digital hygiene during the school assembly — with Diego watching from the back of the room.

### Joaquín — Hijo de Elena, ~35

## PART-BY-PART ARC

### Parte Uno: El Primer Avispa

**Tone:** Re-establish the world with the time jump. Show how characters have (and haven't) changed.

- Morning in 5C. The whiteboard shows Valeria's social energy at 60% — a good start. Laura is on her third coffee, muttering about her intern. Diego has a notification: Emiliano ran his first successful nmap scan last night. Álvaro is prepping for Saturday's D&D session.
- At NovaMind, Laura is assigned to work with Sofía, the new PM. Their first interaction: Laura explains a model in five minutes of technical detail. Sofía summarizes it in two sentences. Laura is stunned.
- Emiliano arrives at Edificio Nexo after school — he has a recurring Wednesday mentorship with Diego. They review what he learned this week. Diego is visibly proud but expresses it as: "Your SYN scan timing improved 23%."
- Meanwhile, Camila (Emiliano's teacher) has been invited to join Álvaro's D&D campaign through Leo. She shows up at the first session. Álvaro notices her immediately and rolls a natural 1 on acting normal.
- Valeria's app project: she is designing the building-wide community coordination app. She shows Elena a prototype. Elena's feedback is brutally honest and useful.
- End of Part 1: Emiliano messages Diego — "algo raro en la red de la escuela. ¿Puedes venir mañana?" Diego's response is a single word: "¿Captura?"

### Parte Dos: El Enjambre

**Tone:** The crisis escalates. Each main character has a moment where their specific skill becomes the right tool.

- Diego visits the school with Emiliano on a Saturday. Confirms it is ransomware — a variant targeting school districts. The attackers are demanding Bitcoin in 72 hours. The school has no backups because the admin disabled them to save disk space (a detail Diego discovers and is quietly apoplectic about).
- The group convenes in 5C as a war room. Álvaro maps the response as a campaign: "Fase Uno: Evaluación. Fase Dos: Contención. Fase Tres: Recuperación. Fase Cuatro: El BBEG." The group assigns roles based on their D&D classes.
- Sofía gets involved because she knows the school board president through a client relationship. She negotiates access to emergency funding for IT consulting — which means NovaMind is now officially involved.
- Joaquín coordinates with the parent association and discovers the ransomware notice was sent to parents as a PDF attachment (the initial infection vector). He helps trace which parents opened it.
- Valeria's app project pivots: the school needs a communication tool to coordinate with parents during the crisis. She builds a prototype in 48 hours. It works. She is exhausted and elated.
- Laura writes a script to analyze the ransomware code — she is not a security researcher, but she knows pattern recognition. She finds something: the encryption algorithm has a quirk that creates a predictable offset. This becomes the key to recovery.
- Camila, at school during the crisis, keeps the students calm and organized. She and Álvaro have one brief moment where she says, "Your campaign planning is actually useful here." He short-circuits.
- End of Part 2: The group has a recovery path, but the school board needs a public statement. Diego is asked to explain what happened at a school assembly. He has never spoken to more than 10 people at once. He says yes anyway.

**Callbacks required in Part 2:**
- The D&D campaign framework is used for actual organization (3+ references)
- Diego consults or updates a flow chart at least twice under pressure
- Laura's pattern-recognition moment surprises even herself
- Valeria's social energy hits zero at least once; she walks away mid-conversation
- Sofía translates something technical into plain Spanish with devastating accuracy

### Parte Tres: La Colmena

**Tone:** Resolution and emotional payoff. The group succeeds together, and each person has a moment of recognition about how they have changed.

- The school assembly: Diego stands at a podium in the auditorium with Emiliano beside him. He explains the ransomware in terms a 14-year-old can understand — because Emiliano helped him write the script. At the end, Emiliano takes a question from a classmate about phishing. Diego watches him answer it. This is the emotional center of the book for Diego.
- Laura's script works — partially. She recovers 80% of the encrypted files. The remaining 20% will take weeks. She tells the school board "80% is not a failure, it is a baseline for iteration." She said that without correcting anyone first. Álvaro notices.
- Valeria's app is adopted by the school district as a pilot program for three schools. The building-wide version runs on a Raspberry Pi in the lobby of Edificio Nexo. Elena is the beta tester. Valeria's moment: she looks at the app running and says, quietly, "Esto es mío."
- Elena's computer literacy workshop graduates its first cohort. She gives a short speech — she has never given a speech. The group is in the audience. She thanks Emiliano for suggesting it. Diego looks like he might cry. He does not, but the effort is visible.
- Álvaro and Camila: after the crisis resolves, she asks if the D&D campaign is still running. He says yes. She says "Bueno. No me había terminado mi personaje." That is all. Nothing romantic happens. It is planted.
- Final scene: the group in 5C, evening. The social energy board reads 100% (it went up, not down). They are decompressing. Joaquín brings takeout that Elena ordered. Emiliano is there, annotated on a laptop. Diego is reviewing the incident post-mortem with him. For the first time, the apartment feels full in a good way.

**Callbacks required in Part 3:**
- "Plomeros digitales" appears one last time — in the school's thank-you banner: "Gracias, Plomeros Digitales"
- Diego's flow chart for "Public Speaking — School Assembly Edition" is referenced or shown

- Laura says something emotionally accurate in technical terms — it is the most honest thing said in the scene
- Valeria checks the social energy board one last time and smiles

## TONE NOTES

- This book is more ensemble-driven than Libro Uno. Each of the four mains gets a distinct arc that involves them helping someone outside the group — teaching, building, organizing, analyzing.
- The crisis (ransomware) is serious but never dark. The humor comes from how the group applies their weird frameworks to it, not from the threat itself.
- Diego's arc is the quiet emotional core. His moment at the school assembly and watching Emiliano answer questions is the scene everything builds toward.
- Valeria's arc is the structural counterpoint — she creates something tangible that exists outside the group. That is her answer to the question she was carrying from Libro Uno.
- Álvaro's romantic thread is a seed, not a flower. It should be barely visible. The reader should wonder if they imagined it.
- Joaquín and Sofía are the "straight men" but not foils — they are genuinely competent people whose competence looks different from the group's. The comedy is in the contrast, not in diminishing either side.
- The TBBT-inspired elements from the source material: the ransomware plot echoes the Bitcoin episode (technical problem with financial stakes), the public-speaking arc echoes Sheldon's Professor Proton audition, and the ensemble coordination echoes wedding-planning-as-crisis-management from S11E09.
